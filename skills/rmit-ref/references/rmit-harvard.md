# RMIT Harvard — rules and templates

Distilled from RMIT Library's **Easy Cite** RMIT Harvard style guide
(https://www.lib.rmit.edu.au/easy-cite/?styleguide=styleguide-1, checked November 2026).
Easy Cite is the authority: if something here disagrees with it, Easy Cite wins, and the
student's course guide or lecturer wins over both.

## Contents
1. Core conventions (read first)

## 1. Core conventions

RMIT Harvard is an author-date style based on the Australian Government Style Manual. It is
close to APA 7th but the punctuation is noticeably lighter:

| Element | RMIT Harvard | NOT this |
|---|---|---|
| Author in list | `Day T`, `Hill AC and Martinez-Diaz L` | `Day, T.` / `Hill, A. C., & Martinez-Diaz, L.` |
| Initials | no full stops, no spaces: `Peterson JR` | `J. R.` |
| Joining authors | `and` (list and in-text) | `&` |
| Authors in list | **all** authors listed | `et al.` in the reference list |
| Year | `(2019)` straight after authors | `2019.` |
| Book / report / webpage title | *italic*, sentence case | Title Case |
| Article / chapter / post title | 'single quotes', sentence case, not italic | "double quotes" |
| Journal name | *italic*, keep journal's capitalisation | |
| Volume, issue, pages | `28(4):686-699` | `vol. 28, no. 4, pp. 686–699` |
| DOI | `doi:10.1080/09650792.2019.1652196.` at the end | `https://doi.org/10...` |
| Online access | `accessed 9 December 2021. URL` | `Retrieved from`, `Available at:` |
| Edition | `2nd edn` | `2nd ed.` |
| Quote page in-text | `(Day 2018:3)` | `(Day, 2018, p. 3)` |
| Comma between author and year in-text | none: `(Papadopoulou 2020)` | `(Papadopoulou, 2020)` |

Titles of online sources are **not** hyperlinked (EndNote compatibility, print).

## 2. In-text citations

- Author family name (or organisation) + year. Page number required for direct quotes if the
  source has pages.
- Information-prominent (end of sentence): `... their practice (Papadopoulou 2020).`
- Author-prominent: `Fiorineschi and Rotini (2021) explain that ...`
- Two authors: always both, joined by `and`: `(Hill and Martinez-Diez 2020)`.
- Three or more: first author + `et al.`: `Haslam et al. (2021)` / `(Haslam et al. 2021)`.
- Direct quotes: single inverted commas + page after a colon:
  `'Building plans tend to ignore climate risks in most instances' (Hill and Martinez-Diez 2020:18).`
  `Day (2018:3) states that '...'.`
- No page numbers: use another locator: `(Shourkaei 2025:Technology section, para. 1)`.
- Block quote: more than 30 words; no quote marks; introduced with a colon; new indented line.
- Changing a quote: `...` for omissions, `[square brackets]` for added words, `(sic)` after
  errors in the original. Keep original spelling.
- Several sources in one bracket: alphabetical, separated by semicolons:
  `(Ghezzi and Cavallo 2018; Jocevski 2020; Soluk et al. 2021)`.
- Same author, same year: `(Ayoob 2021a, 2021b)` — the letters also appear in the list.
- Different first authors with the same family name: add initials: `(Peterson M 2019)`,
  `(Peterson JR et al. 2021)`.
- No date: `n.d.`; estimated date: `c.2021`.
- Unknown author: use the title (italic if the title is italic in the list).
- Pen names: use the pen name: `(Mari 2009)`.
- Secondary citation (use sparingly; try to find the original):
  `Sarkar and Chakrabarti (2008, cited in Fiorineschi and Rotini 2021) suggests ...`
  Only the source you actually read (Fiorineschi and Rotini) goes in the reference list. If
  the primary year is unknown: `(Sarkar and Chakrabarti, cited in Fiorineschi and Rotini 2021)`.
- Organisation abbreviations: if the abbreviation is used in the writing, use it in the
  citation `(WHO 2021)` and in the list as `WHO (World Health Organization) (2021) ...`.

## 3. Reference list rules

- Heading **References**, new page. Only works cited in the text. A **Bibliography** (works read
  but not cited) only if the lecturer asks for one.
- Element order: author → date → title → publisher details → accessed date and URL.
- Alphabetical by first author's family name (organisation names by first letter; no author →
  by title). Same author: oldest first. Same author same year: `2021a`, `2021b`. Different
  authors with same family name: alphabetical by initials.
- `n.d.` no date; `c.` estimated date; `n.p.` no place of publication.
- Little-known or ambiguous place of publication: add the country: `Harlow, England`.
- PDF: link to the page hosting the PDF, not the PDF file itself.
- Include the DOI when one exists, as `doi:...` at the end.
- Non-English works: original-language title followed by the English translation in
  parentheses: `Vietnam Customs (2021) _Thống kê Hải quan_ (Customs statistics), ...`
- Article with no page range but an article number:
  `_Journal of Cleaner Production_, 518:Article 145905, doi:...` (no issue → omit the brackets).
  If both pages and an article number exist: `28:1-18, Article 100791, doi:...`.
- Database sources: give `accessed Day Month Year, Name database.` and no URL (exception:
  WGSN images get both database name and URL).

## 4. Templates by source type

`_underscores_` = italics. Square-bracket hints are instructions, not text to copy.

### Books
- **One author:** `Family Initial (Year) _Title: subtitle_, Nth edn [if not first], Publisher, doi:number OR Place [if no DOI].`
  `Deegan C (2019) _An introduction to accounting: accountability in organisations and society_, Cengage Learning Australia, Southbank.`
- **Two authors:** `Hill AC and Martinez-Diaz L (2020) _Building a resilient tomorrow: how to prepare for the coming climate disruption_, Oxford University Press, New York.`
- **Three or more (list all):** `Mewburn I, Firth K and Lehmann S (2018) _How to fix your academic writing trouble: a practical guide_, Open University Press, Maidenhead.`
- **Organisation as author:** `ICAO (International Civil Aviation Organization) (2018) _Safety management manual_, 4th edn, ICAO, Montréal.` (or full name throughout if the full name is used in the writing)
- **No author:** `_A Warning_ (2019) Little Brown, London.`
- **Edition:** `Day T (2018) _Success in academic writing_, 2nd edn, Palgrave, Basingstoke.`
- **Chapter in edited book:** `Family Initial (Year) 'Chapter title', in Editor Initial (ed/eds) _Book title_, Nth edn, Publisher, doi OR Place.`
  `Sahai S, Goel R and Singh G (2021) 'Building the world of internet of things', in Abraham A, Dash SY, Liang Y and Pani SK (eds) _Advanced soft computing techniques in data science, IoT and cloud computing_, Springer International Publishing AG, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-75657-4.`
- **Edited book:** `Meiner S and Yeager JJ (eds) (2019) _Gerontologic nursing_, 6th edn, Elsevier, St. Louis.`
- **E-book:** same as print; DOI if available, otherwise place:
  `Edwards M, Howard C and Miller R (2020) _Social policy, public policy: from problem to practice_, Routledge, doi:10.4324/9781003117254.`
- **Brochure/pamphlet:** `ACMI (Australian Centre for the Moving Image) (2021) _Disney: the magic of animation_ [brochure], ACMI, Melbourne.`

### Journal articles
- **Template:** `Family Initial, Family Initial and Family Initial (Year) 'Article title: subtitle', _Journal Name_, volume(issue):start-end, doi:number [if available].`
- `Papadopoulou M (2020) 'Supporting the development of early years students' professional identities through an action research programme', _Educational Action Research_, 28(4):686-699, doi:10.1080/09650792.2019.1652196.`
- `Mobbs D, Wise T, Suthana N, Guzmán N, Kriegeskorte N and Leibo JZ (2021) 'Promises and challenges of human computational ethology', _Neuron_, 109(14):2224-2238, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2021.05.021.`
- **No author:** journal name as author: `The Lancet Rheumatology (2021) 'Giant cell arteritis: one size does not fit all', _The Lancet Rheumatology_, 3(12):E819, doi:10.1016/S2665-9913(21)00364-7.`
- **e-journal without DOI, from website:** `..., _Nutrients_, 13(2):516, MDPI Open Access Journals website, accessed 26 November 2021. https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/2/516/`
- **e-journal without DOI, from database:** `..., 24(2):218-234, accessed 26 November 2021, Taylor & Francis Online database.`

### Newspapers and magazines
- **Print:** `Yeates C (6 December 2021) 'CEO pay of big banks could be linked to climate change targets', _The Age_.`
- **No author:** `Financial Review (7 August 2021) 'Small business response to COVID-19', _Financial Review_.`
- **Database:** `Hall J (24 June 2022) 'It's time for a change', _Herald Sun_, accessed 4 July 2022, Factiva database.`
- **Webpage:** `Proust M and Gailberger J (28 November 2021) 'Experts call for ...', _Herald Sun_, accessed 29 November 2021. URL`

### Conference papers
- **Database:** `Udoh E and Getov V (3-5 November 2020) 'Title' [conference presentation], _2020 International Conference on ..._, Sharjah, accessed 13 December 2021, IEEE Xplore database.`
- **Webpage:** `Family Initial (Day Month Year of conference) 'Title' [format], _Conference name_, Place, accessed Day Month Year. URL`
- **Unpublished:** `Garner R and Whitbread R (19-21 October 2021) 'Title' [unpublished conference presentation], _Conference name_, Rotterdam.`

### Websites and webpage documents
- **Template:** `Family Initial OR Organisation (Year) _Title of webpage_, Name of Website website, accessed Day Month Year. URL`
- `Heath N (2021) _What is AI? Here's everything you need to know about artificial intelligence_, ZDNet website, accessed 9 December 2021. https://www.zdnet.com/article/...`
- `WHO (World Health Organization) (2021) _WHO accelerates work on nutrition targets with new commitments_, WHO website, accessed 9 December 2021. URL`
- Homepage, no date: `Telstra (n.d.) Homepage, Telstra website, accessed 13 December 2021. https://www.telstra.com.au/`
- PDF on a website: same template; URL of the page hosting the PDF.

### Social media
- **Blog post:** `Mewburn I (10 June 2020) 'Why academic writing sucks (and how we can fix it)', _The Thesis Whisperer_, accessed 31 November 2021. URL`
- **Post:** `Family Initial OR Organisation (Day Month Year) 'Title or first 10 words of post...' [Tweet / Instagram post / Facebook post], Name of Page, accessed Day Month Year. URL`

### Audiovisual
- **Film:** `Bong JH (director) (2019) _Parasite_ [motion picture], CJ Entertainment, South Korea.`
- **TV episode:** `Taylor A (director) (2017) 'Beyond the wall' [television program], _Game of thrones_ (season 7, episode 6), HBO, New York.`
- **Podcast:** `Fidler R (host) and Rooney C (producer) (19 October 2021) 'Rock and Dave Grohl' [podcast], _Conversations_, ABC, accessed 1 December 2021. URL`
- **YouTube/streaming:** `Creator OR Channel (Day Month Year OR Year) 'Title of video' [video], _Channel or Owning Organisation_, YouTube website, accessed Day Month Year. URL`
- **Song:** `The Beatles (1967) 'Lucy in the sky with diamonds' [Song], _Sgt. Pepper's lonely hearts club band_, Parlophone.`
- **Radio:** `Smail S (presenter) (9 December 2021) 'Title' [radio program], _The world today_, ABC, Melbourne.`

### Images, figures and tables
- **Online image:** `Creator Initial (Year) _Title of image_ [format], Name of Website website, accessed Day Month Year. URL`
- **Database image:** `Primal Pictures (n.d.) _Lateral nasal cavity_ [interactive 3D illustration], accessed 19 August 2022, Anatomy.tv database.`
- **Physical artwork:** `Pollock J (1952) _Blue poles_ [Oil, enamel, aluminium paint, and glass on canvas], National Gallery of Australia, Canberra.`
- **Captions:**
  - own work: `Figure 1: Drawing of a car created by the author in AutoCAD in 2020.`
  - image created by the source it appears in: `Figure 2: A screenshot of the Sephora homepage. Source: Sephora 2023.`
  - image with its own creator: `Figure 1: ... Source: Pollock 1952.`
  - own table/graph from own data: `Table 1: ... Based on the author's own study.`
  - adapted from others' data: `Figure 1: ... Source: adapted from ABS 2022.` (several: `adapted from ABS 2021; DFAT 2021`)
  - reproduced: `Figure 1: ... Source: Fu 2022.`
  - Any source named in a caption also goes in the reference list.

### Reports and data sets
- **ABS:** `ABS (Australian Bureau of Statistics) (2021) _Life tables 2018-2020_, catalogue number 3302.0.55.001, ABS, Canberra, accessed 3 December 2021. URL`
- **Government report:** `Hayne KM (2019) _Title_, Royal Commissions, Australian Government, accessed 13 December 2021. URL`
- **Organisation report:** `AASW (Australian Association of Social Workers) (2021) _Annual report 2020-2021_, AASW website, accessed 13 December 2021. URL`
- **Industry report (database):** `Lo B (2016) _Internet service providers in Australia_, report number J5911, accessed 28 November 2021, IBISWorld database.`
- **Data set:** `RBA (Reserve Bank of Australia) (n.d.) _Consumer price inflation: December 2015 – December 2020_ [data set], RBA website, accessed 10 December 2021. URL`

### Standards and patents
- `Standards Australia (2010) _Residential timber-framed construction - non-cyclonic areas_, AS 1684.2-2010, accessed 9 December 2021, SAI Global database.`
- `Inventor Initial (Year issued) _Title of patent_, Australia Patent 2021105520, accessed Day Month Year. URL`

### Theses
- `Thompson H (2019) _Title_ [master's thesis], RMIT University, accessed 13 December 2021. URL`
- `McLellan JM (2019) _Title_ [PhD dissertation], University of Stirling, accessed 13 December 2021, ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Global database.`

### Other
- **Lecture notes/course material:** `Tran L (2020) 'Managing diverse workforces' [PowerPoint slides, MANG4321], RMIT University, Melbourne.`
- **Student's own previous work:** `Smith J (2021) _An analysis of personality theory_ [unpublished paper for HRHR1001], RMIT University, Melbourne.`
- **Media release:** `Lowe P (2 November 2021) _Statement by ..._ [media release], Reserve Bank of Australia website, accessed 10 December 2021. URL`
- **Personal communication** (interview, email, conversation): **in-text only**, with format and
  full date; never in the reference list because it cannot be verified.
- **Legislation:** act title in italics with year and jurisdiction: `_Child Wellbeing and Safety Act 2005_ (Vic).`
  For detailed legal material RMIT points students to AGLC; check Easy Cite's legal section.
- **Indigenous knowledges, case studies, interviews, encyclopedia entries:** see Easy Cite —
  these have special guidance; do not improvise.

## 5. Generative AI (updated by RMIT February 2026)

- Check first whether the course allows AI at all.
- **Acknowledgement** when AI was used in the process but no AI content is in the final work:
  `I used [tool] (Creator year) to [how it was used].`
  `I used ChatGPT (OpenAI 2026) to provide topic suggestions for my research project.`
  Plus a reference list entry for the tool.
- **Citing AI content:** creator of the tool is the author: `(OpenAI 2026)`.
  - shareable link: `OpenAI (2026) ChatGPT [Large language model], accessed 2 February 2026. https://chat.openai.com/share/...`
  - no shareable link: `RMIT (2026). Val [Large language model], accessed 30 January 2026. https://val.rmit.edu.au/. See Appendix A for prompt and output.`
- **AI image you generated:** caption only, no reference entry:
  `Figure 1: Image generated using OpenAI's DALL-E 2 from the prompt: a brown owl in a forest.`
- AI tools are **not** a substitute for the sources they talk about. If an AI tool mentions a
  paper, the student must find and read the paper and cite the paper — after verifying it exists.

## 6. Common mistakes

1. Using APA punctuation (`Smith, J. (2020).`, `&`, `p. 5`, `https://doi.org/`).
2. `et al.` in the reference list (list every author; `et al.` is for in-text only, 3+ authors).
3. Title Case titles (use sentence case; keep proper nouns and acronyms capitalised).
4. Double quotes around article titles.
5. Missing `accessed Day Month Year` for web sources, or US date order (`December 9, 2021`).
6. Citing Google Scholar, ResearchGate or a database name as if it were the publisher.
7. Comma between author and year in-text, or `p.` before quote pages.
8. List not alphabetical; same-author-same-year entries without `a`/`b`.
9. Citing the source you didn't read (secondary citation done wrong).
10. Reference list entries never cited in the text, or citations missing from the list.
